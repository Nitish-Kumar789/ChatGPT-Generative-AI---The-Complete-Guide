RAG & CAG vs Finetuning

RAG / CAG / Few-Shot Prompting
Knowledge is provided at inference time rather than stored in the model weights.
The relevant data is included in the prompt (directly or via retrieval).
RAG: Uses embeddings and a vector database to retrieve relevant information before generating a response.
CAG: Required documents are loaded directly into the context window.
Few-shot prompting: Provides examples in the prompt to guide behavior; it is mainly for task adaptation, not knowledge storage.
Information must be sent repeatedly whenever it is needed.
Higher token usage leads to higher inference costs.
Easy to update knowledge—just update the documents or context; no retraining needed.
Fine-Tuning
A more complex setup process.
Requires training the base model on task-specific data.
Knowledge or behavior becomes part of the model's weights.
No need to include the same instructions/examples in every prompt.
Lower prompt-token usage and often lower inference cost for repetitive tasks.
Updating knowledge requires additional fine-tuning or retraining.
Best for teaching a model a specific style, format, workflow, or domain behavior rather than frequently changing facts.
Rule of Thumb
Changing knowledge (documents, policies, latest data) → RAG/CAG.
Changing behavior (tone, format, task-specific patterns) → Fine-tuning.
Small number of examples needed → Few-shot prompting.
Frequently repeated instructions causing large prompts → Consider fine-tuning.
